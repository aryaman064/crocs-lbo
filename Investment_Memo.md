# Investment Memo: Take-Private of Crocs, Inc. (NASDAQ: CROX)

**Prepared by:** Aryaman Balyan | **Date:** November 2026 | Hypothetical transaction for practice; not investment advice.

## Recommendation

**Pass at a market premium. Bid only at or below about $164 per share.**

At a 25% premium ($196.10 per share) the base case returns an 11.7% IRR and 1.74x MOIC over five years, well short of a 20% target. The model earns 20% only at about $164 per share, a 4% premium and 8.6x LTM EBITDA, which a public board is unlikely to accept.

## The business

Crocs sells casual footwear under two brands. In the twelve months to 9/30/2026 it earned $4.1B of revenue and $1.0B of Adjusted EBITDA, a 24.6% margin.

- **Crocs Brand** (83% of revenue): $3.4B, growing about 3%.
- **HEYDUDE** (17% of revenue): $0.7B. Revenue fell from $824M in 2024 to $715M in 2025, and the company wrote down $737M of HEYDUDE trademark and goodwill in 2025.

## Why it looks like a buyout candidate

- **Cash generation.** Capital spending is under 2% of revenue, so most EBITDA converts to cash. The model repays $2.4B of debt in five years and takes leverage from 5.5x to 2.7x.
- **Margins.** Gross margin is about 59% and has held through the HEYDUDE decline.
- **Low existing leverage.** Debt is $1.3B, about 1.3x EBITDA.

## Why the returns fall short

- **Price.** A 25% premium means paying 10.0x EBITDA for a business growing in the low single digits.
- **Growth.** The base case has EBITDA rising from $1,010M to $1,168M, about 3% a year. With no margin expansion and no increase in the exit multiple, debt paydown does most of the work.
- **Value bridge.** Of the $3.6B gain, $1.6B comes from EBITDA growth and $2.1B from debt paydown net of fees. Multiple change contributes nothing.

## What would change the answer

| Case | IRR |
|---|---|
| Base: 25% premium, 10.0x exit | 11.7% |
| 10% premium, 10.0x exit | 17.4% |
| 25% premium, 12.0x exit | 17.2% |
| 10% premium, 11.0x exit | 20.4% |

A 20% return needs a much lower price, a higher exit multiple, or a real HEYDUDE recovery. None of these is in the base case.

## Key risks

- **Fashion risk.** Demand for the core clog could fade, and the forecast assumes it does not.
- **HEYDUDE.** The base case assumes the brand stabilises. Further decline would cut EBITDA and exit value.
- **Leverage.** At 5.5x, interest takes about $470M in year one, close to half of EBITDA.
- **Tariffs and sourcing.** Products are made overseas, so trade costs affect margins.
